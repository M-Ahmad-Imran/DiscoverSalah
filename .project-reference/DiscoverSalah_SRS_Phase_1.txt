Abstract and project context
Abstract
DiscoverSalah addresses the difficulty of finding current mosque-specific congregation times and judging whether a journey can finish before prayer begins. It combines maintained mosque records, dated sessions, geospatial discovery, route estimates and transparent timing status. Public users search without an account, select walking or motorcycle travel, and choose nearby or whole-city discovery. Friday and Eid planning supports multiple sessions and distant venues.
The management model links each mosque to a Local Representative and a supervising Focal Person. Representatives submit information; Focal Persons receive account notifications and verify it by calling the registered Imam. The proposed publication policy protects unverified changes while preserving an audit trail. The intended result is a usable and accountable public service, to be validated through actual journeys and mosque operations. No market-size, adoption or field-accuracy claim is made here.
1 Introduction and Background
1.1 Product Vision and Problem Context
Product goal: enable people to find a relevant, maintained congregation and understand the estimated time needed to reach it. Current pain points described in the proposal are scattered timings, local changes, unfamiliar locations and no clear maintenance responsibility. These are project hypotheses pending user research, not findings from a completed survey.
Target users include local worshippers, commuters and travelers who do not know the nearest participating mosque or its Jama’at time. Discovery uses their current or chosen location. The service supports walking and motorcycle journeys, including longer Friday and Eid trips; it does not promise arrival or search along an entire travel itinerary.
User research plan and evidence status
No participant responses or interview records were supplied. Proposed discovery research: a short survey with at least 10 public users and 8 interviews across 3 worshippers, 2 representatives, 2 Imams and 1 potential Focal Person. Recruitment is planned, not completed; record overlap if a survey participant is also interviewed. Observe schedule maintenance at 3 consenting mosques.
Ask how timings are currently obtained, how often they change, preferred travel modes, acceptable verification delay, phone availability, and trust in estimated arrivals. Observe one representative update and one Focal Person review using a prototype. Obtain participation permission, anonymize findings, and record dates, actual counts and limitations. The Product Owner reviews findings before approving uncertain defaults; recruitment failure remains an open dependency.
Expected benefits
Users gain clearer choices; mosque representatives gain a simple update channel; Focal Persons gain manageable review queues. These are intended benefits to measure, not achieved outcomes. Knowledge areas include requirements engineering, geospatial systems, access control, time handling, user experience and operational quality assurance.

1.3 Scope and release boundaries
Core release: anonymous discovery; controlled management accounts; mosque registration with private Imam contact; in-account review notifications; phone verification; recurring/dated timetables; walking and motorcycle estimates; nearby and whole-city scopes; Friday/Eid sessions; reports, reassignment and audit. Begin in one selected city with declared participating coverage. Public missing-mosque suggestions and controlled contributor applications are included in this final Phase 1 scope, as specified in Section 3.5.
Initial release also includes a limited mosque notice board with religious-event, service and verified death/Janazah notices. English/Urdu presentation, future-departure forecasts and automated reminders remain explicit scope decisions. Deferred: subscriptions, payment collection, compensation automation, native apps, car/cycle modes, OCR, third-party messaging, social feeds and nationwide launch.

The launch city, staffing, budget, dates and routing provider are unassigned. An Imam contact can be reached with permission, and each area has an accountable reviewer. These assumptions must be checked. Administrative city membership defines Whole City results; a nearby radius may cross area boundaries.
“Shall” defines required behavior for the proposed implementation. Finalizing this document does not validate estimates or approve a release. Confirmed direction includes motorcycle support, in-account notifications and Imam phone verification, Whole City discovery, and no initial subscription. Numerical thresholds and supporting scope decisions remain in Appendix C.
1.5 Operating model and phased growth
Free launch and future funding
Launch without a subscription or payment barrier for public discovery. Begin with two or three manageable areas and expand only when records remain maintained and reviewer capacity is available. After sustained useful coverage is demonstrated, approach suitable Islamic organizations for funding to support service costs and allowances for Local Representatives and Focal Persons. Funding is not secured, and no payment amount, salary or partnership is promised. Billing, fundraising transactions and compensation automation are outside the initial product.
Maintenance from day one
Each published mosque needs an active representative, a scoped Focal Person and an approved timetable before promotion. Timing maintenance cannot wait for wider coverage or external funding. Document initial voluntary duties, availability and who covers service expenses. Pause expansion if maintenance becomes unreliable; flag affected records instead of presenting unsupported recommendations.
Verification workload and urgent cover
Set review hours and a named backup for each area before onboarding. Size assignments against measured calls and review time rather than an arbitrary mosque quota. A single Imam call may confirm an explicitly listed batch of submission versions; record each decision separately. In-account tasks are the authoritative record. Staff check them at agreed intervals and use a documented manual escalation contact for urgent missed work; automated external messaging is deferred.
Community outreach
Use permission-based Imam introductions around Friday gatherings at an agreed time and mosque notice boards with a QR code and short URL. Link to a maintained mosque page with nearby search. Public search-engine-readable pages and shareable links also help travelers. Paid advertising is not part of the initial outreach plan. Outreach means adoption; travel reachability retains its separate meaning.
Pilot review and future allowances
Review active maintained mosques, overdue reviews, confirmed timing errors, repeat visits in aggregate, correction delays, reviewer workload and operating cost per maintained mosque. Define expansion thresholds from actual pilot evidence. If funding becomes available, agree a budget and transparent duties before allowances start. Pay for accountable maintenance and availability, not the number of edits or approvals; funding must never influence ranking or verification.

3 Functional Requirements
These proposed requirements derive from the corresponding user stories. BR rules in Section 3.3 constrain their behavior. Every FR is mapped to an acceptance check with the same numeric suffix; supporting NFRs apply across all stories.
FR-01  Create scoped management accounts; validate parent role and area on every request; public account creation shall not grant management privileges.
FR-02  Authenticate management users, require initial password change, provide verified recovery, logout and session revocation, and deny inactive accounts.
FR-03  Capture mosque identity, entrance coordinates, city, area, representative and private Imam contact with permitted-use acknowledgment; create a draft and flag potential duplicates.
FR-04  Provide approve, return and reject actions after recorded phone verification; require reviewer scope and exact version; no physical visit shall be mandatory.
FR-05  Maintain versioned daily schedules with effective dates and approved dated overrides in Asia/Karachi; distinguish congregation, sermon and prayer-beginning times.
FR-06  Store proposed and approved versions separately; publish changed times only after the proposed phone-approval policy; suppress recommendations only for affected sessions while pending.
FR-07  Create in-account notifications for submissions and decisions, with retryable delivery, dashboard counts, acknowledgment state, due status and configurable reminders.
FR-08  Escalate urgent or overdue tasks to authorized backup or Admin with auditable delegation; retain pending state until evidence supports approval.
FR-09  Support permission-based location and manual area/pin selection; allow timetable browsing without an origin and require a confirmed origin for personal travel estimates.
FR-10  Provide separate Nearby and Whole City scopes; Whole City shall ignore the nearby radius cap and query participating venues by explicit city membership.
FR-11  Provide next-upcoming and selected-prayer/date discovery; identify prayer and date, exclude started sessions, and label tomorrow explicitly using server-aligned time.
FR-12  Obtain mode-appropriate route duration and distance through a provider adapter or validated local model; label source and freshness and fail honestly when unsupported.
Scope enforcement
Authorization checks shall apply to the record’s actual area and assignment, not just a role or client-supplied areaId. A Focal Person cannot create another Focal Person; a representative cannot approve their own submission. Only an authorized reviewer can publish verified changes.

3.2 Detailed use case flows
UC-04 Verify a new mosque
Preconditions: authenticated Focal Person, assigned area, submitted draft and registered Imam contact. Trigger: registration task appears. Main flow: open the exact version; call Imam; confirm identity, representative relationship and times; record outcome; approve; publish; notify representative. Alternatives: unreachable stays pending; mismatch returns for correction; duplicate is investigated; changed version requires reload. Postcondition: only the checked version is published, with audit evidence.
UC-06 Verify a timing update
Preconditions: assigned representative submits a change against a published schedule. Main flow: validate effective date; create new version and review task; show old/new values; call registered Imam; record result; approve exact version; activate at effective time; refresh results. Alternatives: new edit supersedes old submission; rejection preserves approved history; urgent pending change suppresses only affected session recommendations. Postcondition: approved state and notification work are durably recorded; neither a failed notification nor retry silently loses or duplicates the decision.
UC-10 Search the whole city
Preconditions: city and prayer/date selected; origin optional for browsing. Main flow: query all matching participating venues in the selected city; apply session status filters; paginate results; estimate routes for displayed candidates; label partial ETA loading; allow comparison. Alternatives: no participants produces a coverage message; route service unavailable leaves timings browseable; an outside-city origin remains allowed. Postcondition: a distant matching venue is not excluded by the 7 km nearby cap.
UC-13 Assess reachability
Preconditions: valid origin, mode, selected session and current route estimate. Main flow: resolve effective schedule; assess timing eligibility; compute duration, buffers and margin; assign status; sort by the documented rules. Alternatives: missing/provisional/stale/disputed timing gives unavailable; route failure gives unavailable; congregation already started is excluded from upcoming results. Postcondition: the displayed status is reproducible from recorded inputs and never guarantees arrival.
UC-15 Cancel or change an event session
Preconditions: representative has authority over the event venue. Main flow: submit affected session/date and change reason; notify reviewer; immediately flag an affected confirmed session as under review; verify by phone; approve cancellation or revision. Alternatives: provisional Eid date remains provisional; reviewer unavailable invokes backup. Postcondition: cancellation is an explicit exclusion; recurring fallback cannot resurrect the canceled session.
External confirmation and exceptions
A telephone call is a manual activity; the platform records its outcome, not proof of a completed telecom connection. Reviewers must not self-approve their own submissions. If the same person would occupy conflicting roles, route the review to an authorized independent backup or Admin. Approval after a session has started updates history but cannot restore it to upcoming results.
3.3 Business rules and state transitions
Rule
Proposed behavior
BR-01 Publication
A changed timetable requires recorded phone verification of the exact version before publication. This is a proposed baseline pending owner confirmation.
BR-02 Pending scope
Only sessions and effective dates affected by a pending update lose positive reachability. Unchanged approved sessions remain eligible.
BR-03 Time precedence
Approved dated override/session takes precedence over recurring timing. Cancellation explicitly blocks fallback. Reject conflicting active overrides.
BR-04 Freshness
Freshness is Recent at age ≤7 days, Needs confirmation at >7 to ≤14 days, and Stale at >14 days. An unchanged representative confirmation or approved change starts this clock. Phone verification retains its own date. Reconfirmation cannot clear a dispute, expiry or pending change.
BR-05 Events
A positive Friday/Eid recommendation requires a confirmed date and final phone check within the preceding 48 hours. Earlier planning remains visible as confirmation-needed forecast.
BR-06 Buffers
Proposed minima: walking 5 minutes, motorcycle 8 minutes; users may increase. Add a validated uncertainty allowance separately; never assume zero when uncalibrated.
BR-07 Eligibility
No positive label for missing, expired, provisional, stale, disputed, currently affected pending or unmaintained sessions, or unvalidated/unavailable routes.
BR-08 Ranking
Eligible status group first; recent confirmation before needs-confirmation; route duration ascending; congregation time ascending; stable session ID. No-origin/partial ETA lists sort by time and are not labeled global shortest journey.
BR-09 Geography
Nearby 5 km default, 7 km maximum. Whole City uses explicit city membership with no nearby cap; area boundaries restrict editing only.
BR-10 Lifecycle
Started sessions are not upcoming. Tomorrow is explicitly labeled. Reassignment revokes old rights on the next protected request.
BR-11 Contacts
Registered Imam number is private; replacement needs an established contact or independently verified mosque authority, never self-confirmation from the new number.
BR-12 Escalation
Immediately flag urgent changes; proposed reminder after 30 minutes without action and backup alert at that time or sooner if effective time is near. Ordinary tasks escalate at 24 hours. No-response never implies approval.
Reachability calculation and labels
Margin = session start − departure time − route duration − preparation buffer − uncertainty allowance. For eligible inputs, margin ≥0 means Estimated reachable. If margin <0 but departure plus route duration is before the start, label Too tight. Arrival at or after the start means Unlikely to reach. BR-07 ineligibility takes precedence and means Reachability unavailable. Use server-aligned current time for leaving now, or the selected future departure for a qualified forecast. Calculate before rounding display values.
Submission states: Draft → Submitted → Approved, Returned or Rejected; a newer submission may mark an older pending version Superseded. Approved future schedules wait until their effective time. Listing states are separate: Draft, Published, Suspended or Maintenance pending. Notification Read is separate from review Resolved. These distinctions prevent status changes from falsely implying verification.

Interface behavior
Public APIs return published public fields only; management APIs authorize role and record scope server-side. Invalid inputs return field-specific errors without internal secrets. Concurrent edits return a conflict with a reload path. Retried mutation requests must not create duplicate sessions or decisions. Final endpoint names and schemas belong in the design phase.
Location coordinates use a documented longitude/latitude order. Schedule interpretation uses Asia/Karachi; audit timestamps use UTC. The frontend refreshes when returning from background and at least every 60 seconds while active; server time anchors countdowns. Offline state stops live reachability claims.
Separate adapters cover geocoding, map tiles, walking/motorcycle routing and navigation handoff. Evaluate provider coverage, licensing, quotas, traffic and future-departure support before selection [R4–R5]. Future estimates lacking predictive traffic must say so. Route timeout is proposed at 8 seconds, after which results degrade to timetable-only. No paid provider or tariff is assumed.
Technology baseline and boundaries
Proposed stack remains React, Node.js/Express and MongoDB with geospatial indexing [R4]. The technology choice does not guarantee performance. City search must preserve pagination over all matching venues; progressive route loading must not pretend to be a globally complete travel-time ranking. Operational phone calls and mosque decisions remain outside the software boundary.

5.4 Burndown Chart and Progress Tracking
At sprint start, record selected points P across D working days. The ideal reference line at day d is P × (1 − d/D). Actual remaining work equals selected points minus points for stories satisfying Done; record scope changes separately. Example only: for 15 points and 10 days, ideal remaining points are 15 on day 0, 7.5 on day 5 and 0 on day 10. These are illustrative, not actual progress.
