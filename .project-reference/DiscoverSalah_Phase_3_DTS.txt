Abstract
DiscoverSalah is a community-maintained mosque and congregation discovery website for people at home and while traveling. General prayer calculations do not establish a particular mosque’s congregation time. The service therefore records mosque-specific schedules, approved changes, verification provenance and practical entrance information. A conservative arrival model considers walking or motorcycle travel, preparation and uncertainty; Whole City search supports longer Friday and Eid journeys.
The application is designed as a React web client, a Node.js/Express modular API, a background worker and MongoDB. Immutable proposals, independent approval, scoped assignments and durable review notifications protect timetable integrity. Registered Imam contact information supports remote verification and remains private. The first release is free, with maintenance responsibilities established from launch. Later funding discussions with Islamic organizations do not introduce subscriptions, donations or payroll into the present build.
This phase specifies architecture, interfaces, detailed decision rules, the 15 supplied core UI pages, correction priorities, test cases and a conditional delivery plan. Prototype inspection is complete for documentation purposes; software execution results, routing calibration and independent review require later evidence. The intended outcome is a controlled pilot whose public claims match the quality of its data and operating capacity.
Evidence and change control
Requirements take precedence over illustrative screen text. Conflicting prototype text is logged in Section 4.3 and must be corrected before acceptance. Parameters explicitly labeled proposed require Product Owner approval and field evidence. New scope must update the SRS, this specification and corresponding tests together.

1 Introduction
1.1 Product
DiscoverSalah is a custom browser-based information service. Anonymous visitors select an origin, prayer and date, then compare participating mosques and congregation sessions. Management users maintain those records through controlled submissions and verification. It is not a prayer-calculation authority, a transport guarantee or a mosque attendance system.
1.2 Background
Prayer-time calculators address astronomical prayer windows; map directories address places and navigation; local notices and messaging groups communicate mosque-specific changes. DiscoverSalah combines location discovery with accountable local timetable maintenance. Its proposed distinction is the verification and publication workflow, not an unproven claim of better coverage or accuracy than other products. No institutional previous-project catalogue or comparative field study has been established for this phase; neither is presented as evidence.

1.4 Scope
Included: location selection, list/map discovery, venue details, recurring and exceptional sessions, registration, roles and assignments, draft/review/publication, durable notifications, issue reports, missing-mosque suggestions, contributor applications and moderated expiring notices. The 15 core pages are the initial design set; supporting forms and error states still require implementation design.
Excluded: subscriptions, payments, payroll, advertisements, native mobile apps, automatic Imam calling, automated religious judgments, car or bicycle reachability, OCR timetable extraction and multi-stop journey planning. English and Urdu switching is included. Automated user reminders and future-departure planning are deferred; required in-account submission and decision notifications remain included.
1.5 Business goals and conventions
Business goals
Begin with any willing mosque with management permission, assigned data maintenance and an independent Focal Person; do not wait for three complete areas. Keep public discovery free. Grow through Imam announcements at Friday prayer and mosque notice boards or QR links. Monitor maintenance workload and verification delays so expansion does not outpace local support. Funding discussions with Islamic organizations may follow demonstrated coverage and costs; no funding or payment to contributors is assumed.

The 15 October 2026 decisions in Section 7.4 select Google Maps and Routes API, bilingual launch, private contacts, controlled recovery and owner-led operations. Remaining prerequisites are listed in Section 7.3. A city outside the pilot can be selected only when coverage is explicitly available; the system must not imply nationwide completeness.
2 Technical architecture
The selected design is a custom modular monolith. Online requests handle search, registration, editing and review. A small worker handles durable notifications and scheduled maintenance. Operational counts are limited reporting; no analytical warehouse is needed for the pilot.
Figure 2.1  Proposed runtime architecture retained from the Phase 2 baseline
The browser communicates with the API over HTTPS. Public reads use safe projections; management writes authenticate and authorize each request. MongoDB transactions commit domain changes, audit and outbox records together. External maps, geocoding and routes remain separate adapters. Hosting and provider selection are open procurement decisions, not deployed infrastructure.

2.2 Component interactions and collaborations
Figure 2.4  Submission transaction and notification handoff
Submission uses an idempotency key and expected head revision. In a single transaction the service checks authority, stores the snapshot, supersedes the prior pending version where applicable, advances the head, creates the review task and writes audit/outbox records. A response lost after commit can be retried without creating another proposal. A changed payload with the same key is rejected.
2.2.1 Verification and publication
Figure 2.5  Independent verification and exact-version approval
The Focal Person calls the registered Imam; a displayed phone number or ticked checkbox alone is not evidence of a completed call. Record who was contacted, when, the verified scope and outcome. Unreachable or ambiguous contact leaves the task pending or returns it for correction. Future-effective approval does not publish early.

2.4 Technology architecture
Use React and TypeScript for the responsive browser interface, Node.js/Express with TypeScript for the API and worker, and a MongoDB replica set for transaction support. These are design selections; pin supported versions and dependencies when the repository is initialized. The supplied standalone HTML is a UI reference, not the production React implementation.
Host the website on Vercel. The Product Owner initially owns monitoring, support, backups and recovery. MongoDB remains a separate transaction-capable database; its hosting and backup mechanism still require selection. Adapt API execution to the chosen deployment and use a durable scheduled or queue-driven runner for outbox work; do not assume a permanent process inside a request function. Keep staging separate and secrets server-side. Internet access is needed for clients and chosen providers; offline editing and offline verification are not promised. Size the pilot from load measurements, not invented user forecasts.
2.5 Architecture evaluation
A modular monolith reduces deployment and operational overhead compared with microservices. MongoDB fits document-shaped immutable proposals but requires deliberate indexes, transactions and integrity checks; PostgreSQL is a credible alternative with stronger relational constraints. Retain the agreed database unless an implementation review establishes a concrete benefit from changing it. A worker and outbox avoid the initial cost of a broker while preserving durable handoff.
Use Google Maps for mapping and Google Routes API with WALK and TWO_WHEELER. Validate local routes, restrictions, licensing, privacy, quotas and costs before enabling positive estimates. Do not substitute a car route and relabel it motorcycle. Until a suitable mode is validated, show the timetable and clearly unavailable arrival estimates.

3.1.1 Session and reachability decisions
Resolve an exact prayer/date/session before routing. Apply valid dated overrides and cancellations before recurrence. Multiple Friday sessions are separate choices; ordinary Dhuhr is not silently added unless explicitly confirmed. Eid dates may remain provisional and an outdoor venue requires its own entrance. Khutbah time is separate from congregation start.
Condition in precedence order
Public behavior
Missing, expired, provisional, stale, disputed, affected pending or unmaintained session
Show appropriate timing status; reachability unavailable, with reason.
Unsupported route mode, route failure or unvalidated uncertainty
Timetable remains visible; arrival estimate unavailable.
Raw arrival at or after session start
Unlikely; never suggest speeding.
Raw arrival before start but full allowances do not fit
Tight; explain insufficient margin.
Arrival including preparation and uncertainty at or before start
Reachable estimate, with time and assumptions; no attendance guarantee.
Margin = session start − departure time − route duration − preparation allowance − uncertainty allowance. Proposed minimum preparation: 5 minutes walking, 8 minutes motorcycle, adjustable upward. Mode-specific uncertainty must be field-validated; zero is not an assumed default. Illustrative test: start 13:30, depart 13:00, route 15, preparation 5, uncertainty 5 gives +5 minutes. These fixture values are not field calibration results.
Refresh active results at least every 60 seconds and on resume using server-aligned time. Rank predictably by eligibility group, freshness, travel time, session time and stable ID. Do not claim city-wide fastest ordering from a partially routed batch. Use stable base pagination by session time and ID, and identify the subset evaluated for travel.
Nearby uses the proposed default 5 km and maximum 7 km; Whole City uses participating city membership without the 7 km cap. An origin outside that city may still be used. The city’s geographic scope does not imply every mosque has been onboarded.

3.3 Human interface rules
Use plain labels: Walking, Motorcycle, Jama’at time, Last phone verification and Representative reconfirmation. Keep approved values distinct from pending proposals. Explain why a result is unavailable. Never use color alone for status or put private Imam contact details on public pages.
The supplied design uses Inter with 16 px body text, 24 px line height, forest green, mint surfaces and white cards. Normalize the production tokens against the earlier design brief before implementation: primary #146B4A, background #F7FAF8, text #172B23, secondary #52635B, warning #8A4B08 and error #B42318. Screens in this document retain the supplied palette. Target 48 px controls, visible keyboard focus, meaningful labels, logical headings, 200% zoom and mobile reflow. Contrast and assistive-technology behavior must be tested; conformance is not yet established.
The public flow must work without an account. Invite-only management sign-in resolves permissions from the server. A contributor application is a request for consideration, not self-assignment. Add confirmation only for consequential actions such as approval, reassignment and revocation, and preserve entered data when validation fails.
3.4 Maps and bilingual behavior
Use Google Maps and Google Routes API for distinct walking and motorcycle modes. Confirm the real entrance pin during onboarding. Never relabel car or bicycle routing as motorcycle. Show Estimated reachable, Time is tight or Arrival estimate unavailable with reasons and assumptions; no arrival or attendance guarantee.
Google documents walking and two-wheeler routes as beta and requires a warning about potentially incomplete paths. Display the applicable provider warning and attribution where routes are shown. Motorcycle calls have higher billing rates. Keep restricted routing credentials on the server and apply provider quotas, application request limits and billing alerts; alerts alone do not enforce a spending cap.
Route a bounded, visibly identified subset of eligible results. Whole City still lists participating matches through stable pagination, including unrouted results labeled not evaluated. Do not equate unevaluated results with unreachable results or claim the fastest result across a partially evaluated city. On provider failure or usage-limit exhaustion retain timetable and map/list discovery with arrival unavailable.
Field-test walking and motorcycle allowances separately before enabling positive estimates. Preserve the preparation and uncertainty model in Section 3.1.1. Navigation opens only on explicit user action and uses the verified entrance; disclose the handoff to the external provider.
English and Urdu launch requirements
Provide a visible language switch on public and management pages. Persist the preference locally; keep the current page, selected prayer/date, origin, filters and unsaved form content. Translate labels, errors, status explanations and core help; do not automatically translate names or alter entered timetable values.
Use Urdu right-to-left layout and an Urdu-capable font. Keep phone numbers, email addresses, URLs and numeric time fields isolated in the correct direction. Test mixed-language content, mobile reflow, keyboard navigation and all 15 core pages in both languages. The supplied screenshots remain English prototype references; Urdu layouts require implementation and acceptance testing.

5 Other design details
5.1 Security and privacy
Use opaque server sessions in Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookies with CSRF protection and origin checks for state-changing requests. Rate-limit authentication and public submissions; use safe generic login errors. Admin access requires MFA. Authorization evaluates role, active account, scope, assignment revision and no-self-approval on the server. Recovery and retention follow Section 5.1.1. Session and invitation expiry settings must be configured and tested before launch.
Allowlist public fields rather than deleting known private fields after serialization. Keep registered Imam numbers and verification notes available only to authorized management. Avoid logging passwords, tokens, precise origins and full phone numbers. Limit contact collection to purpose and permission; do not collect CNIC by default. Restrict audit access and make app-level history append-only.
5.1.1 Privacy retention and account recovery
Visitors can browse, search and navigate without signing in. Request location only when needed and offer manual entry. Use precise origin coordinates transiently for the query; do not create a location-history record or include coordinates in application logs. Explain the transfer to Google when requesting routes; provider processing is separate from our retention policy.
Store the Imam verification number privately with permission to contact. Permit access only to assigned personnel who need it and authorized administration. A public mosque office contact is a separate optional field with explicit publication permission. Changing private contact details requires independent evidence; a new number cannot verify itself.
Management accounts are individual and invitation-only. Use verified email for password recovery. Reset tokens are securely generated, stored hashed, single-use and expire after 15 minutes. Rate-limit requests and use the same response for known and unknown emails. On successful reset, invalidate existing sessions and notify the account holder. Do not automatically log the user in.
If email access is lost, the Product Owner checks identity through an existing trusted mosque contact before changing recovery details. Record the evidence and decision; a newly supplied number alone is insufficient. Require admin MFA and offline recovery codes. Never transmit shared or replacement passwords to staff.
Record
Retention and deletion rule
Unsuccessful or withdrawn applications
Delete personal contact details 90 days after rejection or withdrawal. Review unresolved applications at 90 days; do not retain them indefinitely without a documented purpose.
Security logs
Delete after 90 days; exclude passwords, tokens, private phone details and precise visitor coordinates.
Schedule changes and approval history
Retain for 12 months after the recorded action with minimal actor identifiers. Keep currently effective schedule and necessary provenance while active; do not delete the live timetable merely because it is old.
Backups
Encrypted daily backups, rolling 30-day retention, restricted access. Test restoration before launch. After restore, reapply deletion records before serving data.
Active contacts and other personal records
Keep only while needed for the active role or service. Remove obsolete contact details when their purpose ends. Review closed reports/notices for removal or anonymization; retain no unnecessary personal narrative.
Retention deletion is an authorized, auditable process distinct from routine append-only history. Preserve minimal deletion markers long enough to prevent backup restoration from reintroducing deleted data. These are accepted operational defaults, not a claim of statutory retention requirements.
5.2 Reliability and operations
Approval commits independently of notification delivery. The worker retries with leases, bounded backoff and deduplication; repeated failure raises an operational alert. A delayed activation worker cannot expose future timings early because reads check effective instants. A revoked or missing maintenance assignment makes the affected scope maintenance-pending and blocks positive arrival claims until coverage is restored.
Proposed operational targets are 99.5% availability, daily backups, RPO 24 hours and RTO 4 hours. Daily encrypted backups and a 30-day rolling backup retention are accepted. Availability, RPO and RTO remain planning targets until the separate database setup and restore rehearsal establish feasibility. Monitor API errors, route timeouts, oldest review age, stale coverage, outbox failures and assignment gaps; route initial alerts to the Product Owner and document a trusted emergency handover path before pilot.
5.3 Freshness and event governance
Proposed reconfirmation/freshness thresholds of 7 and 14 days and final event verification within 48 hours require owner approval. Store separate timestamps for representative reconfirmation and independent phone verification. A changed proposal or dispute affects its exact prayer/date scope, not every unaffected session. Expired or provisional event information must not remain positively ranked.
5.4 Announcements and growth
Moderated notices cover religious events, service information and death/Janazah announcements with source authority and consent appropriate to the content. Publish minimal necessary information, provide correction/cancellation and enforce expiry on reads. Janazah route feasibility is deferred unless separately specified. The team visits mosques to obtain permission and verify initial listings. After activation, use a management-approved poster with a website link and mosque-specific QR code, and Imam announcements. Social posts invite Focal applicants; review and scoped invitation precede access. No paid placement changes ranking.
6 Test specification and results
6.1 Test strategy and entry criteria
Testing begins with pure schedule and arrival rules, then API integration against a transaction-capable database, UI journeys and recovery/security checks. Provider contract tests use deterministic stubs; field journeys validate actual route uncertainty separately. No stub result establishes real-world route accuracy.
Entry requires a versioned build, approved test configuration, isolated staging, transaction-enabled database, synthetic accounts/venues and controlled provider responses. Record commit/build ID, environment, parameters, tester, execution time, evidence path and actual results for every run. Do not use real Imam phone numbers in automated fixtures.

Decision
Accepted implementation baseline
Mosque-led pilot
Any consenting mosque may start; management permission, an assigned maintainer, a registered Imam contact and an independent Focal reviewer are required. No minimum of 2–3 complete areas.
Recruitment and promotion
Founder/team visits mosques. Social posts recruit candidates; verify before invitations. Publish the mosque-specific QR poster only after the listing is active.
